# M1 frontier — quality versus active parameters under exact whole-group omission

Status: **measured, negative result**. H1 of the registered contract is rejected
for static whole-group omission on Qwen3-1.7B. This file reports what was
measured, against which receipt, and what it does and does not establish. The
contract was registered before any measurement (`docs/EXPERIMENT-CONTRACT.md`,
commit `dad6619c8514cb4a52b3420c007a47fc43e1b47e`, 2026-11-14T21:06:27Z).

This is a distributional-fidelity result, not a capability benchmark. A high KL
means the sparse model's next-token distribution differs from the dense
teacher's on the measured text. It does not measure code generation, arithmetic,
reasoning or factuality.

## Registered threshold

At realized active unique parameters `<= 1e9`, a configuration is **acceptable**
only if the held-out wikitext KL is at most the registered KL bound, the PPL
ratio is at most the registered ratio bound, and top-1 agreement is at least the
registered agreement bound, with the paired-bootstrap 95% CI satisfying the same
bounds. The numeric bounds are fixed in `docs/EXPERIMENT-CONTRACT.md` section 9
(0.05 nats/token; 1.02; 0.95). They were not derived from these results.

## Cost of the intervention

Every arm's receipt records the realized group mask, the active parameter
count, the derived FFN matmul FLOPs per token (`6 * active_intermediate *
hidden` per layer), and CUDA-event forward seconds with the forward count. The
mask application is included in the timed region. These are cost observations
for this Python harness, not a latency benchmark: there is no warm/cold
control, no interleaved schedule, no concurrency and no hardware-counter FLOP
measurement. They are in `frontier.json` under `arms[].timing` and
`arms[].active`, and in the raw sweep receipts. The greedy receipts also record
the selection cost and every candidate evaluation, because the offline oracle
is not deployable and its cost must not be confused with a runtime cost.

## Answers to the mandated questions

- **Is there a `<=1e9` configuration that preserves quality within the
  pre-registered threshold?** No. The best compliant arm misses the KL bound by
  more than two orders of magnitude. H1 is rejected for static whole-group
  omission at this granularity on this teacher.
- **Where is the knee?** Above the ceiling, between 15/16 and 16/16 of the FFN.
- **How much does non-uniform allocation buy over uniform?** The registered
  exactly-matched test buys 1.3% with a CI that includes zero (K3 fails). A
  near-matched additive greedy buys 23% on wikitext and about 10-12% on
  code/math, but at a destroyed operating point and with a 0.5% budget
  difference. Allocation helps; it does not help enough.
- **Does calibration-domain overfitting destroy the gain under shift?** No.
  About half the wikitext gain survives on code and math. Domain shift is not
  the reason the approach fails; the ceiling is.
- **What is the backbone minimum for 1.7B?** The always-active backbone takes
  about two thirds of the ceiling and the affordable FFN share is just under a
  third of the FFN; the exact receipt-sourced figures are in
  `docs/BUDGET-MATH.md`. Quality is not preserved anywhere in that region.
- **Is the 1.7B teacher the right base?** Not for compliant static group
  omission. Its backbone leaves too little FFN budget, and the FFN it can
  afford is far below the level at which the model remains itself. A smaller
  compliant model plus external capacity, or a retraining/upcycling branch that
  teaches the model to work with a sparse FFN, is the remaining path. This
  experiment does not test either.

## What this does not prove

- It does not prove that no sparse method works. It bounds **static,
  token-independent, whole-group omission at sixteen contiguous groups per
  layer, with no retraining and no learned routing**.
- It does not test dynamic per-token selection, causal utility selection,
  gradient-based attribution, interaction-aware selectors, or trained sparse
  models (MoSE/UpIT-style upcycling). Those are the next hypotheses, not
  conclusions of this one.
- It does not measure capability, latency, throughput or physical I/O. FLOPs in
  the receipts are derived from realized channel counts, not hardware counters.
  Wall-clock is in the receipts as a cost of the intervention, not as a
  benchmark.
- It does not establish that wikitext KL predicts downstream task quality. A
  model can have low KL and still fail a task, and vice versa. The KL here is so
  large that the direction is not in doubt for these arms, but the
  generalisation from KL to capability is not established.
