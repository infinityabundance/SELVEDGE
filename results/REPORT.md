## Established

1. **A pinned teacher oracle exists and runs offline after one documented fetch
   step.** The oracle court image pins Python, torch (CUDA 13.2 build),
   transformers, tokenizers, safetensors and numpy. A real CUDA matmul and SiLU
   ran inside the container under device passthrough; the probe and receipt record
   the device and the `--gpus all` fallback reason.
2. **Two teachers are acquired, licensed and hash-pinned.** Qwen3-0.6B and
   Qwen3-1.7B at fixed commit SHAs, Apache-2.0, with every file's SHA-256 and
   size in `models/MODELS.lock.json`. The license text is reproduced verbatim in
   `docs/MODELS.md`.
3. **Exact accounting on real safetensors.** `selvedge inspect-model` reads the
   real headers, rejects unclassified tensors, verifies the embedding/output tie
   byte-for-byte, and reports component counts, the always-active backbone,
   removable FFN, ceiling arithmetic and FLOP estimates.
4. **The 1e9 ceiling is not binding for Qwen3-0.6B and is binding for
   Qwen3-1.7B.** Qwen3-4B's backbone alone exceeds the ceiling, so it cannot be
   a compliant teacher; it is deliberately not downloaded.
5. **A real checkpoint converts to group shards and reconstructs exactly on real
   activations.** The default grouping is sixteen contiguous groups per layer,
   stored as f32; the shard count and payload bytes are in each receipt. The
   grouped-from-bundle path and the dense-from-checkpoint path agree bitwise on
   every compared component.
6. **The f32-equivalent teacher computation is reproduced within a documented
   bound**, and the native bf16 output is reproduced when the teacher's rounding
   sequence is emulated explicitly. Both checks pass with zero violations.
7. **The masked-MLP instrument is exact on the measured paths.** With every
   group selected it is bitwise equal to the unmodified dense f32 teacher on
   real held-out windows. An independent Rust grouped sum over the published
   bundle agrees with the Python grouped reference on a real layer and a real
   four-group subset within the registered f32 bound.
8. **The quality-versus-active-parameter frontier is measured, with receipts.**
   Static uniform, magnitude, frequency, whole-layer and greedy allocations
   were evaluated on a pinned, hash-split held-out set with paired bootstrap
   CIs. Every arm at or below the ceiling is far outside the registered
   acceptable and usable tiers. The curve is a cliff near the fully dense end.
9. **The static result is negative and reproducible.** Two independent complete
   runs produced bitwise-identical per-arm aggregates. Failed attempts,
   amendments and the dirty-worktree superseded receipts are retained.
10. **The per-token oracle potential is measured and negative.** A causally
    unavailable oracle that chooses an exact local subset per token and per
    layer, at the same realized budget, was evaluated at 16, 64 and 4 groups.
    The 64-group oracle is the best and is still far outside the registered
    usable tier. A bounded exact-KL refinement does not rescue it. Its bound is
    about logical omission quality, not a compliant runtime.
11. **Per-token selection helps logically but cannot escape the cliff, and the
    best arms are physically non-compliant.** At matched realized active
    parameters, per-token activation magnitude beats the best static M1 arm, and
    every other causally available per-token arm is worse than per-token
    magnitude. The learned unary, learned interaction-aware,
    gradient-attribution and corrected-attribution arms are all worse. Corrected
    accounting shows that the magnitude, frequency and learned selectors read all
    gate/up channels to score groups, so they cannot be executed under the
    ceiling; only static/random masks executed through the sliced sparse path are
    physically compliant, and they are far worse. See `docs/M2-CORRECTION.md`.
12. **The Rust Atlas boundary is implemented and parity-tested.**
    `selvedge-atlas` owns the quality-observation record, the interaction
    graph, the leakage-bounded selector input, seven selectors, the two-view
    head contract and independent matched-budget accounting. `selvedge atlas
    fit|select|compare|cost` run; the Python/Rust parity, leakage, graph and
    calibration tests pass on exported real fixtures.
13. **Exactness and the two verdicts are type-level, not prose.**
    `ExactSelection` requires a `NativeSetWitness` from full set equality
    against a declared `NativeComputationSet`; the old string reclassification
    is gone, and compile-fail cases prove the barrier. `CausalAvailability` and
    `PhysicalCompliance` are separate evidence-bearing records; there is no
    `deployable` boolean. `ParameterLedger::admit_selection_path` checks the
    union of selector and execution parameters before execution. See
    `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` and `docs/M2-CORRECTION.md`.
14. **The tiered store and its exact-mode reads are measured.** `selvedge-store`
    implements the host-RAM cache policies, scoped reads, advisory prefetch and
    the `prefetch_bytes_wasted` metric. Cold/warm whole-bundle reads on the real
    0.6B and 1.7B bundles, the page-cache control, read-size, queue-depth,
    alignment, checksum and cache-residency sweeps are three-repeat measurements
    with ranges and CVs in `docs/STORAGE.md` and the `results/store` and
    `results/io` summaries.
15. **A trace-driven execution simulator exists, is calibrated from those
    measurements, and is validated on held-out arms.** `selvedge-sim` owns a
    versioned execution trace that can only describe what was selected, an
    empirical cost model (with exponential-versus-lognormal residual evidence),
    a discrete-event engine with coalescing, backpressure, cancellation,
    deadlines, execution envelopes and a two-timescale working set, and a
    placement study whose layouts are ranked by the simulator. Calibrated on
    `plan-order` and `lru-1of4`, it replayed three other 0.6B arms and one 1.7B
    arm with **zero hit/miss mismatches and an exact device-byte prediction**;
    the stall discrepancies are in `docs/SIMULATOR.md`. The live
    simulator-versus-store comparison agreed exactly on bytes and hit counts and
    agreed on the p95/p50 shape, but its absolute latency check failed because
    the host was saturated by the T2 pilot; that is recorded as inconclusive, not
    as a result. No GPU transfer is simulated, and no trained-router trace
exists, so all locality conclusions are conditional on the trained model's
    routing statistics.

### What was decided, and what it means

- **`H*` vs `H` (K15) fails.** Error-directed routing did not produce a
  decisive gain over the naive hybrid at this scale on either endpoint, so WS3's
  central bet is recorded as a negative result. No bank scaling follows, and the
  mechanism is not carried into WS4/WS5 without a new pre-registration.
- **`H*` vs `D` (K16) fails.** The error-directed hybrid is *worse* than the
  equally-trained dense adapter on the primary composite, and its KL is worse
  too. Conditional capacity did not beat dense capacity of equal active size at
  this budget.
- **`X` vs `D` (K18) fails** despite a positive composite difference, because the
  pooled KL regressed by more than the registered margin. This is the
  conjunctive rule doing its job: an apparent task gain paid for with a
  distributional loss is not a win.
- **`M` vs `D0` (K17) passes**, the one positive: the hashed memory path closes
  most of the frozen-reference gap on the decisive statistic while holding active
  parameters equal.

Two things this does **not** say. It does not say error-directed capacity is
impossible — it says it is not supported at 0.6B active / 67M tokens / one seed.
And it is one seed per arm: no seed-level interval is claimed, and `D_M` and `HL`
are registered and unrun.

**No arm scored a verified task.** Every arm's output was either unparseable
(`invalid_solution`) or failed to compile (`compile_error`); one `H` and one `H*`
item compiled and then failed a hidden behavioural test, which is the one place
the instrument demonstrably reached the model's output end to end. The reference
solutions pass the same checkers, so the court is not broken — a 0.6B distilled
model asked to emit complete replacement files for multi-file Rust crates with
hidden tests is simply outside this instrument's reach. S28's failure taxonomy
(§"The diagnosis" below) replaces this paragraph's inference with a per-item
classification: the format, not only the model, is the binding constraint, and
the result is an instrument floor rather than a ranking.

That makes the verified-task comparison **uninformative rather than negative for
`H*`**: a floor effect cannot rank arms, so it must not be read as "the hybrid is
no better at Rust tasks". It is also a *different* result from the KL/PC endpoint
above, and the two are reported separately (P14.6): the KL numbers are
distillation measurements, the capability numbers are correctness measurements,
and no cache or systems number is presented as either. The per-arm table, the
per-category intervals (all degenerate at the floor) and the undefined relative
differences are in `results/capability/runs/t2-comparison.json`; the power
analysis that says this suite could not resolve a 10% effect even with a
non-zero baseline is quoted in §"Capability court" below and in
`docs/CAPABILITY-COURT.md` §7.1.

The measured floor also invalidates one assumption of that power analysis: it
models a 0.5 baseline success rate. The real baseline here is zero, so the
variance term it used is optimistic and the effective power is *lower* than the
reported figure. Closing this is P14.8's job, and the first thing it must do is
estimate item difficulty from actual solver runs rather than assume a baseline.

**Not changed, and this is the part the next session runs on.**

### The routed-overflow audit, kept separate from the verdict

The P12.1 audit found a real defect in the shared dispatch: the capacity
**validity mask is built in expert-sorted pair order and applied after the pairs
are restored to token order**, so a pair is gated by another pair's validity
whenever any expert overflowed. The finished routed arm's own training log shows
that overflow is routine, not a corner case, and the toy reproduction shows an
overflowing pair reading another token's activation.

What this report therefore says about provenance, and nothing more:

* every routed arm (`X`, and `H`/`H*` as they finish) was trained and is being
  evaluated by a dispatcher whose overflow mask is misapplied;
* the correction is `mask_order="pair"`, **off by default**, recorded in every
  run receipt, so no registered arm, threshold or comparison has changed;
* the correction, its reproduction, the measured overflow rate and the list of
affected receipts are in `docs/P12.1-DISPATCH-AUDIT.md` and
  `results/t2/dispatch-audit-20261115T234953Z.json`, and the follow-up runs that
  measure whether the verdict moved are registered as P12.1a.

No T2 quality number appears in this section, because the pilot's own numbers are
in `results/T2-VERDICT.md` and the audit is not a verdict either: it is a statement
about which code produced which receipt.

### The plan's dependency graph was too wide, and three "questions" were published

WS19 treated the Rust kernels as a gate on the whole program. The reference
forward is validated, so the gate is narrower: the **accounting** half of P19.0

sentence in `docs/PRIOR-ART-AND-NOVELTY.md` §18.3, and three wider sentences are

## M9 — the update was below the grid, and the graft was supervised on the wrong quantity (S42)

This section records what S42 established before its GPU runs finished; the
measured scores and the run state are appended below it as `M9.1`, from their
receipts.

**The bar arm's weight update was numerically inert.** The full-backbone arm
holds bf16 weights with no fp32 master copy. bf16 stores 7 mantissa bits, so at
the registered 1e-4 learning rate an update is below half an ulp of the weight
and nearest-even rounds every step to zero. `Adam8bit` now applies the exact fp32
update to the bf16 grid with unbiased stochastic rounding by default, in bounded
chunks so the transient stays tens of MB. Two variants are registered beside it:
`nearest` (the old behaviour, as a control) and `compensated` (nearest plus a bf16
residual carry, one bf16 element per trainable element, bytes reported by the
optimiser). The registered synthetic tests show a 0.49-ulp update changing about
half of the elements under stochastic rounding and exactly none under
nearest-even, and 1000 steps of 0.1-ulp updates moving the nearest-even weight by
nothing while the compensation buffer tracks the exact total inside about one
ulp. On real steps the optimiser reports the bitwise unchanged fraction, the
round-to-nearest fraction and the sub-half-ulp fraction, so "the fix is working"
is a measurement in the run receipt rather than a claim in this document.

**The representation graft was supervised on reconstruction, and the measurement
says so.** On the 2M-token held-out slice the step-0 teacher graft costs +0.57475
nats of CE against base, while the ungrafted step-0 module is bit-identical to
base (quoted from `results/stage2/s41-heldout-comparison.json`, stamp
`20261117T035128Z`, revision `73f9574fc7ec23e8cc82a5a971a8423509e6d086`). P19.2b
replaces it with a corrective graft: a correction gate initialised to exactly
zero so an untrained graft reproduces the base bit for bit, supervision from the
teacher's advantage over the base rather than from mapped teacher states, and a
preservation weight that penalises a KL increase where the base already predicts
the teacher well. The gate's gradient ordering (non-zero at zero, rows learn
after it opens) is tested rather than assumed, as are the bit-exact parity and
the interference ordering.

**What is registered, not proven.** `docs/ws19-corrective-graft-preregistration-v1.json`
registers P19.2b's four arms (grafted-corrective, grafted-representation,
adapter, gate-zero control) at 2M tokens with the same teacher, stream and
active union (537,601 against 537,600: +1 gate scalar per site), and P19.8's
objective comparison (CE+KL against advantage-weighted correction) with a
four-branch decision rule and a next route per branch. Neither has been trained
for a score.

**What this does not establish.** Not that the corrective graft beats the
representation graft; not that stochastic rounding beats the compensation buffer
at this budget; not that the corrective objective beats CE+KL; and nothing about
the 20M-token bar, which is a separate registered run. The literature verdicts
and their limits are in `docs/S42-LITERATURE.md`.

## S46 — what a token gate can recover, and what a cheap gate actually recovers

The question the next mechanism depends on is not "is the memory damage real" (S45
measured it) but "could *any* gate recover it, and does a gate that needs no
second forward recover any of it". `scripts/s46_oracle_gate.py` measures the base
and memory branches per completion position on a fixed, selected damage sample
with CPU forwards, and derives three upper bounds from that one forward receipt:
a per-token oracle (deliberately non-causal), a class oracle, and a one-threshold
cheap-feature oracle. The class and feature gates are fitted on one half of the
prompts and evaluated on the other half. A second pass decodes the same prompts
greedily under five gate policies and grades the final answer, because a
likelihood bound is not an answer-level result.

The sample is selected for damage (base correct, memory-2m wrong), so nothing
below is a benchmark score and none of it substitutes for the anchored table. The
probe's own tests are `oracle/tests/test_s46_oracle_gate.py`; the numbers are

`results/stage2/s46-oracle-bounds-*.json` and
`results/stage2/s46-oracle-generation-*.json`.
