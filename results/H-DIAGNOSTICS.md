## Reading of these numbers

Every value below is pulled from the receipt; the *sentences* are interpretation and are labelled as such. None of them is a causal claim, and none replaces the registered T2 verdict.

1. **The two single-path arms' routers are close to the same matrix.** `H`'s and `X`'s routers start from one draw and end up with mean cosine **0.9509** to each other. Each also collapsed onto almost one direction: top-1 energy share **0.7892** (`H`) and **0.7935** (`X`) of eight, with mean |cos| between expert directions **0.7626** and **0.7674**. Two consequences follow, and they are measurements rather than guesses: the eight specialists are nearly interchangeable to the selector, and `H` and `X` route almost identically despite `H` carrying a memory path that `X` does not.
2. **`H*`'s router is not the failure.** Its usage entropy is **0.9969** of the maximum and its top-4 set entropy is **10.5567** bits against the utility's own **10.6708**, with no dead expert in any layer. The router reproduces a near-uniform target, so any deficiency is in the *target* (near-uniform labels, which `docs/T2-DIAGNOSTICS.md` measures from the same artifact) and not in the head's capacity to fit it.
3. **In `H` the memory path is open but writes little; `M`'s writes several times more.** On identical stored inputs, the memory write per unit input RMS is **0.0064–0.0105** for `H` and **0.0602–0.4769** for `M`, a ratio of **9.3686×** to **45.6065×**. `H`'s gate is *not* suppressed (gate mean **0.9938** at the last insertion): the gate opens and the projection under-writes. Read as an interpretation, this is dilution of two paths that each need the whole task gradient, not composition.
4. **The table is not the constraint.** The calibration slice touches **0.1522** of each table's rows, but over **8388608** stream tokens the read set covers **1.0000** of it, and every row is updated by training. The `2^18` rows are sized for the budget, so a memory-side explanation of the hybrid's loss cannot rest on table capacity or on unused rows.
5. **`H` keeps most of what both arms solved and little of what only one solved — and the dense control behaves the same way.** On the items both `X` and `M` solved (`n = 256`) `H` scores **0.8099**; on the items only one solved it scores **0.2774** and **0.3190**, while `D` scores **0.2909** and **0.2302** on the same cells. The single-path gains therefore do not transfer to another parameterisation at all: they are arm-specific, and `H`'s shortfall is a broad reduction rather than interference aimed at the other arms' successes.
Together these point at three separate facts rather than one: the selector in the distilled arms is close to constant and near-identical across arms; the hybrid writes less through each path than the single-path arm writes through its only path; and the item-level gains of a single-path arm are largely not transferable. The forward-pass measurements deferred below are what would separate cause from correlation, and they are not run here.

## What this establishes

1. Whether `H`'s losses are concentrated on the items one of the single-path arms solved and the other did not. That is the item-level form of the question and it is answered from receipts.
2. How far each arm's router moved from the shared initialisation, and whether its expert directions stayed separable or collapsed onto one or two directions. This is measured from weights against a reconstructed initialiser with a wrong-seed control.
3. What `H*`'s router does to real inputs: usage, entropy, dead experts, and the spread across positions, from stored states.
4. What the memory path reads, how concentrated those reads are on the calibration slice, and how large each arm's memory path is relative to its bank on the same inputs.
5. Whether expert choice and memory-row popularity are coupled at the insertion layers, with a null.

## What this does not establish

- Weight displacement from a reconstructed initialiser is exact only for the parameters the optimiser initialised that way; every router and memory tensor is, and the wrong-seed control is recorded in each row.
- A small router direction is a necessary, not sufficient, condition for an unused expert: usage is measured separately in section 3 and the receipt keeps the two apart.
- Path magnitudes are computed from saved modules on saved hidden states, without capacity dispatch. The deployed path drops overflow pairs (H's evaluation receipt records a dropped pair fraction), so these magnitudes are the module's own scale, not the deployed mixture.
- The row-usage measurement is a property of the addressing function and the calibration slice, identical for every arm that shares the geometry. It says which rows the path reads, not which rows matter.
- Mutual information between expert choice and row popularity is a dependence measure on one arm's calibration states. It cannot distinguish 'the memory causes the routing' from 'both are driven by the token', which is why the permutation null is reported with it.
